How I think

Seven rules I keep

Titles say where someone sat. These say how I decide. Each rule has a business side and a technical side, because I don't separate the two. Every rule comes with a receipt you can check.

Start with the number that hurts.

Business
Find what the problem costs, who pays for it, and who would pay to make it stop. No number, no project.
Tech
That number becomes the spec. It decides what the system optimises and what it must never break.

The company I'm building

trovehaul.ai

The intelligent way to trade with India.

Cross-border sourcing still runs on phone calls, spreadsheets and trust built one deal at a time. Trovehaul starts as a trader, runs every shipment itself, and turns each one into data that trains an AI sourcing platform.

The problem

A buyer abroad can't easily tell which Indian supplier is reliable, what a fair landed price is, or whether the bank details on an invoice are real. Each answer costs days and some risk.

The bet

Run real trades first. Every order teaches the system what was quoted, what arrived and what went wrong. Software built from that record beats software built from guesses.

Why I own it

It joins everything I've built: scoring with hard limits, reconciliation that checks money against records, agents that escalate instead of guessing. Trade is where those habits pay.

Supplier Scoring

Ranks suppliers on audits, quality, on-time loading and claims, so orders go to the best record, not the loudest seller.

Building
Live Pricing

Landed-cost quote in minutes from market prices, freight, FX and duty, with a guardrail that blocks quotes below margin.

Building
Fraud Checks

Verifies buyers and suppliers, screens sanctions lists and flags changed bank details before money moves.

Building
Auto Documents

Invoice, packing list and certificate of origin drafted and cross-checked from one order record.

Planned
Tracking

Follows each container door to door and raises an alert the moment something slips.

Planned
Market intelligence

Reads global trade data to choose where we sell. The other five sit on top of it.

In use
NowA trader with AI tools

We run every trade ourselves, starting with the Gulf and Europe. Each shipment trains the system.

NextAn open trading desk

Buyers get live quotes, tracking and documents in one portal. Suppliers see their score and demand from abroad.

LaterAn AI trade network

Any buyer sources any Indian product, with pricing, compliance and logistics handled by the platform.

Export registrations are in progress. Prices are indicative until then. We will not call ourselves the exporter of record before we are one.

Transparency

What didn't work yet

Every project has a gap between what it claims and what it does. Here are mine, with what I'm doing about each.

29/30

My LLM judge flagged 29 of 30 ReconIQ investigations as overreach, including correct ones. The judge was wrong, not the agent: it lacked the tool documentation the agent had.

Fix: give the judge the same tool semantics, then trust it as a quality gate.

9 cases

Nine exceptions get a different root-cause label between runs when the evidence itself is ambiguous. The decision to escalate never changed, but the label did.

Fix: majority vote across samples for those case types only.

3/30

Only 3 of 30 Tier 3 cases had ground truth to verify against. They were all correct, but 10% coverage is too thin to call the tier proven.

Fix: grow the labelled set before claiming accuracy.
